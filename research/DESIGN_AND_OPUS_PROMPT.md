# Thoracic model for simulated spirometry by ancestry group

## Purpose

The goal is a mechanistic model that predicts FVC, FEV1, TLC, and RV from anthropometry and physiologic priors alone, without fitting to any spirometry. The output is compared with GLI-2012 group differences only afterward. The question is how much of the observed between-group FVC gap at matched age, sex, and height can be reproduced from body proportions and chest geometry. The same engine can also generate large synthetic cohorts for the educational PFT tool.

## Structure of the model

A universe is one draw of every parameter from its prior. The Monte Carlo runs thousands of universes, and each universe yields a deterministic mean function of group, sex, age, and standing height. Group ratios at matched age, sex, and height are computed within each universe, so the output is a distribution of ratios and not a single number.

The mean function runs in this order. Sitting height is standing height times a sitting height ratio that is higher in childhood and converges to a group-specific adult value as the trunk finishes growing after the legs. Thoracic cavity volume is an elliptical cross-section (breadth, and depth as a fraction of breadth) times thoracic height (a fraction of sitting height) times a shape factor. Chest breadth scales with standing height and with the sitting height ratio raised to an exponent (`iso`) between 0 and 1, where 0 means chest width is independent of trunk length and 1 means full isometry with trunk length. TLC is a fraction (`kappa`) of cavity volume. RV/TLC rises with age from a prior intercept and slope. FVC is TLC minus RV. FEV1/FVC falls with age and carries a group offset that stands in for dysanapsis (airway caliber relative to lung size).

Individual-level simulation adds adult height drawn per group and sex, a growth curve with group-specific timing, and residual variation on TLC, RV/TLC, and FEV1/FVC. TLC, FVC, FEV1, and RV are then derived so that FEV1 ≤ FVC ≤ TLC holds by construction.

## What the first run showed

With placeholder priors, the group ratios are almost flat across age and sex, because the group effects enter multiplicatively through sitting height ratio and chest offsets, and growth timing offsets are small. The median AFR/EUR FVC ratio is about 0.94, driven mainly by the lower sitting height ratio raised to a power of roughly 1 + 2·iso. The 95 percent interval runs from about 0.81 to 1.09, and nearly all of that width comes from the chest breadth and depth group offsets, which were given mean zero and a 3 percent standard deviation because no non-spirometric source for them has been loaded yet. The NEA and SEA ratios come out above 1 (about 1.02) because their sitting height ratio prior is higher than EUR, whereas GLI shows lower FVC in these groups at matched height. The model as specified therefore predicts the wrong direction for the Asian groups, which means either the sitting height prior, the missing chest-size data, or a structural omission (for example, lung-to-cavity fraction differing by group) needs attention. These are prior-predictive results from unreferenced numbers, so none of it is evidence.

## Known weaknesses to address

1. Every numeric prior is a placeholder. Each needs a cited source with a non-spirometric origin (anthropometric surveys, CT thoracic and lung volumes, autopsy series).
2. The chest dimension offsets by group carry most of the uncertainty and have no data behind them. CT-derived thoracic cage dimensions by ancestry are the key missing input.
3. Group ratios are age-flat, which does not match the observed pattern, where the gap emerges around puberty and changes with aging. Growth timing, differential trunk and leg growth, and age-related changes in chest wall compliance and respiratory muscle strength are underspecified.
4. FEV1/FVC differences by group are generated by an arbitrary mean-zero offset. Dysanapsis needs an anatomical prior (airway lumen area from CT relative to lung volume).
5. Within-group variance is a constant lognormal term. Real variance changes with age and height, and the correlation structure between volumes is not specified.
6. Socioeconomic position, nutrition, and air pollution affect body proportions, so anthropometric inputs are not purely ancestral. The model cannot separate these.
7. The ratio of means ignores that GLI equations use a spline for age and a power of height. Comparison should use GLI predicted values at the same age, sex, and height rather than reconstructed curves.
8. Agreement with the observed gap would not establish mechanism. The informative result is the residual gap the model cannot explain.

## Validation plan

Parameter priors are fixed and documented before any comparison. The GLI-2012 predicted FVC and FEV1 for each group are exported with `rspiro` on the same age, sex, and height grid, and the ratio to EUR is compared with the model posterior ratio. The fraction of the gap explained is reported as a distribution. Sensitivity analysis (for example, rank correlation or Sobol indices of each parameter against the ratio) shows which priors drive the result and where better data would help most. Cohort output is checked for physiologic constraints (zero violations expected), for internal consistency (FEV1/FVC distribution, TLC against FVC and RV), and eventually against institutional PFT data with TLC and DLCO.
