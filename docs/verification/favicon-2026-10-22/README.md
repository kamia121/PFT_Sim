# Favicon redesign, 2026-10-22

The previous favicon, an inline data-URI SVG repeated in `index.html`, `report.html` and `quiz.html` (kept here as `previous.svg`), drew a single teal hump on the navy tile. At tab size it reads as a generic bell curve or the letter n and does not identify a pulmonary function tool. The replacement is a stylized flow-volume loop, the one tracing that every pulmonary function report carries and that a clinician recognizes from its outline alone. It deliberately leaves out text and axes: neither survives 16 px.

## Candidates

Three candidates were drawn on a 32-unit grid, so that every unit is half a device pixel at 16 px and 1 px at 32 px. Each was rendered in Chromium at device scale 1 at true 16, 32 and 48 px, on white and on the light and dark tab-strip colors of Chrome and Firefox, beside a mock tab and with nearest-neighbour magnifications of the 16 and 32 px rasters (`contact-sheet.png`, regenerated by `node docs/verification/favicon-2026-10-22/contact-sheet.mjs` from the repository root).

Candidate 1 (`candidate-1-outline.svg`) is the loop drawn as a 3-unit teal outline on the navy tile, with a mitered apex at peak expiratory flow. At 32 and 48 px it is a recognizable loop. At 16 px the 1.5 px stroke closes the interior, and the shape collapses into a D or guitar-pick outline in which the expiratory peak no longer stands out.

Candidate 2 (`round1-sailboat.svg`, shipped in the first round and replaced in the second, see below) fills the two limbs as separate shapes on the navy tile: the expiratory limb in teal (`--mon-current`, the current-trace color of the monitor panels), with a steep rise to a sharp peak expiratory flow at about 23 % of the vital capacity and a slightly convex descent to residual volume, and the inspiratory limb as a half-ellipse in periwinkle (`--mon-snap`). A 2-unit navy gap between them is the zero-flow axis. It falls exactly on pixel row 9 at 16 px and therefore renders as one crisp dark line, not as an antialiased smear. The expiratory height (13.5 units) is about 1.9 times the inspiratory depth (7 units). That exaggerates a normal tracing, on which peak inspiratory flow approaches peak expiratory flow, and the peak sits somewhat later in the vital capacity than it usually does. Both departures keep the expiratory peak legible at 16 px. Filled shapes keep their silhouette at 16 px where strokes do not. The two limbs also separate by hue as well as by the axis gap. The tile carries contrast on light and dark tab strips alike.

Candidate 3 (`candidate-3-tileless.svg`) is the outline without a tile, stroked navy under `prefers-color-scheme: light` and teal under dark. It uses the full 16 px for the glyph and suits both themes in browsers that honor media queries inside SVG favicons. It shares candidate 1's collapse into a D at 16 px. The ICO and Apple fallbacks also cannot adapt to the theme, so the icon would differ between browsers.

## Weaknesses of the first-round design (candidate 2)

At 16 px the two-tone loop can also be read as a sailboat or a half-sunk disc. The resemblance to a flow-volume loop depends on the left-skewed peak and on the bowl below the axis, and it is weaker for viewers who do not read these tracings. The navy tile has low contrast against the darkest tab strips (#1C1B22 and #202124). The tile edge is then barely visible and the icon is carried by the teal and periwinkle fills, as with the previous icon.

## Second round: one closed outline

Rendered at 128 px, candidate 2 reads as a sailboat, a teal sail over a periwinkle hull, because the two limbs are separate filled shapes divided by a gap. A flow-volume loop is recognized by one continuous contour, and the gap removes it. The first round's candidate 1 kept the contour but its 1.5 px stroke closed the interior at 16 px and the shape became a D. The second round keeps one closed outline and makes the interior legible by tinting it, so that a 1 px stroke around a tinted interior still reads as a loop at 16 px.

Four variants were drawn on the same 32-unit grid (`round2-candidate-e.svg`, `-f.svg`, `-h.svg` and the selected `assets/icons/favicon.svg`, candidate G). All four use one stroked path with a single subpath, closed by `Z`, in `--mon-current` at 2 to 2.2 units (about 1 px at 16 px), over a `--mon-current` tint (30 to 42 % opacity; candidate G, 22 % over the loop and 30 % more over the expiratory lobe) on the `--mcw-navy` tile. The left edge, right edge and zero-flow axis lie at units 5, 27 and 17, so that a 2-unit stroke covers exactly one pixel at 16 px and renders without antialiasing. The expiratory limb now descends with the concave shape of a normal tracing (flow falls quickly after the peak and tapers toward residual volume), where candidate 2 descended convexly. The peak sits at about 18 % of the vital capacity, and the inspiratory limb is a broad arc whose depth (about 8 units at its lowest point) is about two thirds of the expiratory height (12 units).

Candidate G, selected, adds a second tint over the expiratory lobe only, on top of a tint over the whole loop. The step in tint falls on the zero-flow axis and shows at 16 px as a darker lower band, so the viewer sees where expiration ends and inspiration begins without a second shape or a second hue. Candidates E and F tint the interior uniformly and read equally well as a closed loop at 32 px; they were rejected because at 16 px they lose the axis cue and read as a leaf or a shark fin. Candidate H widens the loop to units 4.5 to 27.5, which crowds the tile edge at 16 px.

Limits of this check. It is a visual judgment on one rendering engine (Chromium, device scale 1), not a measured recognition test with viewers. The outline still resembles a leaf or a guitar pick for viewers who do not read these tracings, and the second tint is subtle on the darkest tab strips (#1C1B22 and #202124), where the tile edge is also barely visible. Candidate 2 is kept as `round1-sailboat.svg` for the record, and `tests/favicon.test.mjs` now asserts that the loop is a single closed stroked path so that it cannot revert to two separate shapes.

## Files and links

`assets/icons/favicon.svg` is the source. `tools/make-favicons.mjs` rasterizes it with the pinned Playwright Chromium into `assets/icons/favicon.ico`, which holds PNG-encoded 16, 32 and 48 px images with transparent corners for browsers without SVG favicon support, and into `assets/icons/apple-touch-icon.png`, an opaque 180 px square whose corners are filled with the tile navy because iOS applies its own mask. Each entry page links the three files in the order ICO (`sizes="32x32"`, so that Chromium prefers the SVG), SVG, then Apple icon. The build copies `assets/` unchanged, and `scripts/serve.mjs` now serves `.ico` as `image/x-icon`. `tests/favicon.test.mjs` checks the links, the files, the SVG colors against `tokens.css`, the ICO directory and the Apple icon header.

The previous icon was an inline data URI, so the new file URLs cannot collide with a cached copy. GitHub Pages serves them with its default ten-minute cache lifetime. A later redesign should change the file names or add a version query to the three `href` values, because browsers keep favicons in a separate, longer-lived cache.

The downloadable single-file report written by `src/report/html.js` has no favicon, before or after this change. It is opened from disk, where a relative icon link would not resolve, and it was left unchanged.
