# PFT_Sim: technologist statements and test-quality scenarios (research catalog)

Teaching use only; not for patient care. Compiled 2026-10-20.

## How to read this document

- The "statement a technologist might write" column is illustrative wording written for this catalog. It is not quoted from the ATS/ERS operator-comment list (Section E11 of the 2019 spirometry standard), which could not be retrieved.
- "mem" in the Source column means that part of the row comes from the author's memory or physiological reasoning and was not seen in a source during this research. Treat those parts as unverified.
- Confidence: high = the principle or number appears in a primary document that was read in full text (S1, S5); medium = well-established mechanism or found in a secondary/search-result summary; low = from memory, single small study, or sources conflict.
- Arrows: up = higher than the true value, down = lower. "~" = little or no change. "ratio" = FEV1/FVC.
- Access limits: WebFetch could not resolve any host (DNS failure), so only two primary documents were read in full text, through the PubMed MCP full-text tool: the ATS/ERS 2019 spirometry standard (S1) and the ATS 2023 race/ethnicity statement (S5). Everything else comes from search-result summaries or PubMed abstracts. Verify against primary text before encoding numbers in `SPEC.md`.

## Key facts verified in primary text (S1, ATS/ERS 2019 spirometry)

- Avoid before testing: smoking, vaping, or water pipe within 1 h; intoxicants within 8 h; vigorous exercise within 1 h; clothing that restricts chest or abdominal expansion. A heavy meal and caffeine are not in the table.
- Bronchodilator withholding times (Table 8): SABA 4-6 h; SAMA 12 h; LABA 24 h; ultra-LABA 36 h; LAMA 36-48 h. ICS and leukotriene modifiers need not be withheld. If the question is response on current therapy, do not withhold.
- The operator must record drug type, dose, and time of last dose, and must record deviations from the preparation list.
- Relative contraindications (Table 2): acute MI within 1 wk; unstable PE; uncontrolled pulmonary hypertension; syncope with forced expiration or cough; eye surgery within 1 wk; sinus or middle-ear surgery or infection within 1 wk; brain surgery within 4 wk; pneumothorax; thoracic or abdominal surgery within 4 wk; late-term pregnancy; active or suspected transmissible respiratory infection including TB; hemoptysis, significant secretions, oral lesions or bleeding.
- Birth sex, not gender identity, is the determinant of predicted lung size; patients should be told this and offered the chance to state gender identity. If birth sex or ethnicity is not disclosed, the operator note must alert the interpreter and state the default used.
- GLI ethnicity categories (2012): white, African American, Northeast Asian, Southeast Asian, other/mixed. GLI is the default reference set.
- Height: without shoes; for patients unable to stand, ulna length (preferred in children, measured with calipers) or arm span (Section E6, not retrieved). Remeasure height at each visit unless age is 25 y or older with a reliable prior measurement in the same facility within 1 y.
- Acceptability: back-extrapolated volume (BEV) must be at most 5% of FVC or 0.100 L, whichever is greater (2005 standard: 0.150 L); no cough in the first second (FEV1 only); no glottic closure; end-of-forced-expiration (EOFE) indicator (plateau at most 0.025 L in the last 1 s, or time 15 s or more, or FVC within tolerance of or greater than the largest prior FVC); no leak; no mouthpiece obstruction; FIVC minus FVC at most 0.100 L or 5%; 10-90% PEF rise time at most 150 ms. No minimum forced expiratory time is required.
- Repeatability (age over 6 y): two largest FVC within 0.150 L and two largest FEV1 within 0.150 L. Grades A-F and U (usable) are assigned separately to FEV1 and FVC.
- Stop if FEV1 falls below 80% of the starting value. Up to 8 maneuvers in adults.
- Bronchodilator response uses "bronchodilator responsiveness" wording. A post-BD maneuver coded as pre-BD (or the reverse) must be correctable; elapsed time must trigger a warning; an apparent FVC change can reflect a change in forced expiratory time.
- Patients with upper-airway obstruction or neuromuscular disease may exceed the BEV limit; the operator must be able to override automatic acceptability flags.
- Pause at TLC of 4-6 s, or slow inspiration, lowers PEF and FEV1; keep the pause to 2 s or less.
- Restrictive disease and young patients may empty the lung in under 6 s and show a convex terminal flow-volume pattern that must not be mistaken for early termination.

## Flow-volume loop signatures (for the loop renderer)

| Problem | Expiratory limb signature | Inspiratory limb | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Slow or hesitant start | Rounded, delayed, lower peak; rise time over 150 ms; large BEV on volume-time trace | Normal | S1 |
| Submaximal effort | Low, rounded peak; limb shape can still look normal in descent; peak differs between blows | Often smaller | S1, S25 |
| Early termination (stop, inspiration, off mouthpiece) | Curve ends abruptly at a nonzero flow, FVC truncated | Present or absent | S1, S25 |
| Cough, first second | Sharp spike(s) or notch near the peak and early descending limb | Normal | S1, S25 |
| Glottic closure | Abrupt cessation of flow (sharp downslope to zero) mid-expiration | Normal | S1, S25 |
| Leak at lips or nose | Irregular, early fall-off, low FVC; may look like early termination | Often smaller | S1 |
| Tongue or teeth in front of mouthpiece | Sudden flow drop or erratic plateau-like step; low PEF possible | Variable | S1 (mouthpiece obstruction), shape mem |
| Zero-flow error | Under- or overestimated volume; drift of volume-time trace | Variable | S1 |
| Started below TLC | FIVC larger than FVC (inspiratory limb extends past expiratory start) | FIVC minus FVC over 0.100 L or 5% | S1 |
| Restrictive or high-recoil lung | Convex descending limb, ends in under 6 s, plateau under 1 s; valid | Normal | S1 |
| Variable or inconsistent loops | Overlaid blows differ in peak and shape; poor repeatability | Varies | S1, shape mem |

## Teaching-point shortlist for the simulator (cross-cutting)

1. Low FVC with a normal ratio is not restriction until TLC is measured and the maneuvers are shown to be acceptable (QS-02, QS-04, QS-09, QS-13, SPEC-19, PLE-06).
2. Large BEV hides obstruction by inflating FEV1 (QS-01).
3. A different reference equation can flip a classification without any change in the patient (SPEC-12, SPEC-13, SPEC-15, SPEC-17).
4. A negative BDR is meaningless if baseline bronchodilators were not withheld or if post-BD effort or timing was poor (PRE-01 to PRE-03, QS-17 to QS-19).
5. Raw DLCO is not diagnostic without Hb, COHb, inspired-volume, and breath-hold checks (SPEC-20 to SPEC-22, DLC-01 to DLC-10).
6. High KCO with low VA can be technique (DLC-01) or chest-wall/extraparenchymal disease; the report must show VA.

## Unverified or conflicting

### Sex, gender, and the choice of reference equation (contested)

- Current ATS/ERS guidance (verified in S1): use birth sex for the predicted lung size, inform the patient, and let them state gender identity. The standard gives no rule for patients on gender-affirming hormones, for non-binary patients, or for intersex patients.
- The evidence base found is small. One retrospective study (S7, n=160, subjects with airflow obstruction, 80 born male and 80 born female) showed that the sex used changes percent predicted by about 12-18 percentage points and altered the interpretation in 45% (born male) and 70% (born female); the presence of obstruction (ratio below LLN) did not change. It was not designed to say which sex is correct.
- A 2026 Western Australian study of 56 participants is reported only in a news item (S10). The news item says birth-sex equations were accurate for lung size, but opposite-sex values may fit DLCO better. No paper, effect sizes, or hormone-duration data were seen. Treat as unverified.
- Mayo trial NCT05587751 (S11) is designed to find the time at which normative values should move toward gender identity; no results were seen.
- Hormone effects on hemoglobin (testosterone raises, estrogen/anti-androgens lower) change DLCO adjustment targets; no source found on which Hb reference (male or female) to use in the Hb adjustment formula for a transgender patient. The formula in S22 has different constants for males and females.
- The Foer 2021 letter (S8) and Roth 2023 article (S9) could not be read, so any position they take is unknown here. The simulator should present the choice and its effect on z-scores as a teaching contrast and avoid asserting a single correct rule beyond "ATS 2019 says birth sex".

### Race and ethnicity fields

- The 2019 standard (S1) still lists GLI-2012 group names; the 2023 ATS statement (S5) recommends GLI Global (race-neutral average) for spirometry and no race adjustment for volumes and DLCO. The ERS co-endorsement is from a search summary, not a primary page read here.
- GLI Global is a weighted composite, not race-agnostic (S5). Regions and groups are underrepresented. Implementation effect found only through a search summary: among Black patients in a US center, race-neutral equations raised the prevalence of restriction. That claim, and a separate claim that GLI Global had a similar fit to GLI-2012 "other" but wider limits of normal (S6 abstract), should be checked.
- GLI TLCO (2017) and GLI volumes (2021) are described in search summaries as based on White/European ancestry (S29). Spirometry (GLI Global) and DLCO/TLC therefore use different reference populations, which can create discordant classifications.
- Handling of South Asian and Middle Eastern patients in GLI-2012 ("other/mixed") is from memory and not verified.

### Patient preparation numbers

- Smoking: S1 says 1 h before spirometry; a secondary summary said 4 h; patient handouts range from 4 h to 24 h or "day of test"; the NHLBI page says day of test. For DLCO, the 2017 standard asks patients to refrain on the day of the test and to record time of the last cigarette (search summary); a 24 h abstinence rule was not found in the 2017 summary text and the AARC 1999 guideline lists smoking within 24 h as a relative contraindication.
- Exercise: S1 says 1 h; a secondary summary said 30 min.
- Meals: not in the S1 avoid table; handouts say 2 h.
- Caffeine: not in S1; handouts range 4-6 h; Cochrane evidence is from asthma trials.
- SABA hold: S1 gives 4-6 h; several clinical-trial protocols use 6-8 h; some lab handouts use up to 48 h for all inhalers.
- Bronchodilator hold times for bronchial challenge are longer than for BDR testing (S1 footnote); not detailed here.

### Spirometry acceptability numbers

- BEV: S1 uses 0.100 L or 5% FVC; the 2005 standard used 0.150 L; teaching sheets still print 0.150 L.
- FEV1 acceptability for children 6 y or under uses FEV0.75; GLI covers it for 3-7 y (S1).
- Whether submaximal effort can mimic obstruction was not supported in the sources found; early termination and low effort mostly raise the ratio. The catalog states effects accordingly.
- Numerical effect sizes (for example, how many mL a given BEV adds to FEV1, or how much a 5-s TLC pause lowers FEV1) were not found beyond direction statements. The simulator must choose its own parameters and label them uncalibrated.
- The z = -1.645 LLN rule and the severity bands in S4 were not verified here; the PubMed abstract and search summary did not show them.

### DLCO

- Inspired volume threshold: executive-summary text says at least 90% of the largest VC, with at least 85% inhaled within 4 s; older standard and the user's brief used 85% (S2, S21). Verify in the full 2017 document.
- Repeatability: one study-guide says 2 mL/min/mmHg (single absolute tolerance), AARC/ATS older text says 3 mL/min/mmHg or 10%. Unverified.
- Breath-hold: 2017 says 10 +/- 2 s; AARC 1999 says 8-12 s. Calculation method (Jones-Meade) in older text; the direction of error with long breath-hold depends on the formula and is uncertain.
- Hemoglobin: the 1.7 Hb/(10.22 + Hb) males and 1.7 Hb/(9.38 + Hb) females and children under 15 y expressions come from a manufacturer note, a trial protocol and slides (S22), and apply to the predicted value in the 2017 standard. The "0.6 Hb" form in the brief was not found.
- COHb: "about 1% DLCO per 1% COHb" was not found in the sources retrieved; do not encode without checking the standard. The 2017 standard's text says endogenous COHb of about 1-2% is already in the reference data (search summary).
- Altitude: no verified 2017 formula; a manufacturer note gives a PIO2-based correction only for supplemental oxygen; slides cite roughly 0.35% DLCO change per mmHg of PAO2 and an optional predicted-value altitude correction. Chronic altitude residents have higher DLCO; acute exposure data differ. GLI TLCO may underpredict at altitude even after adjustment (S23).
- Obesity: one PLoS One study found a significant inverse linear relation between BMI and DLCO with means still normal; a review table lists DLCO as unchanged in simple obesity. A common teaching statement that DLCO rises with obesity was not supported by the sources found.
- Pregnancy: one review says DLCO is unchanged; teaching often says it falls in late pregnancy. Conflicting; not resolved.
- Repeat-test interval (4 min) and a five-test maximum are from memory and were not confirmed.
- Dentures: S1 reports conflicting studies (2001 better with dentures; 2018 FVC 0.080 L higher without).

### Plethysmography (2023 standard)

- Panting frequency: slides give 0.5-1 Hz target, 1.5 Hz concern; AARC 2001 (retired) gives about 1 Hz; another snippet gives a reject threshold of 1.5-2.0 Hz with minimal obstruction. Exact 2023 thresholds were not read.
- FRC repeatability: 5% in one slide deck, 10% for the mean in a vendor sheet. Unresolved.
- Thermal drift acceptance limit: none found beyond "drift loops are on the reject list".
- Direction of error for mouth leak and shutter leak is from reasoning, not from a source.

### Height estimation

- Arm span: adult divisors 1.03 (men) and 1.01 (women) are relayed by a search summary of the ERS SpirXpert page; a Polish review cites 1.06; the ERJ 2011 paper reports ratios between about 1.01 and 1.04 for men and 1.00 and 1.02 for women and notes that earlier proposals of 1.06 differ. Ratios vary with sex, age, and ethnic group; regression equations outperform fixed ratios in a Korean study. The ATS 2019 supplement Section E6 equations were not retrieved.
- Ulna: child equations exist (Gauld 2004; one 2018 child equation reads height (cm) = 0.59 x ulnar length (mm) + 13.1), but no verified adult ulna-to-height table was obtained. Half-arm-span and ulna estimates overestimated predicted values in one Portuguese study.
