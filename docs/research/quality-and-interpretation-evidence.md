## Spirometry quality and interpretation accuracy

Quality of routine spirometry varies widely with setting and criteria. Hegewald (Ann Am Thorac Soc 2016, 27598295) [AB] found 60% of primary-care spirograms acceptable and only 1 of 17 office spirometers within accuracy criteria, with an error that would recategorize 28% of tests from obstructed to non-obstructed. Eaton (Chest 1999, 10453871) [AB]: after training 13.5% of tests met full ATS criteria (3.4% untrained), failures mostly at the end of the test. Leuppi (Respiration 2010, 19786731) [AB]: 29,817 general-practice tests with automated grading, grades A to D and F at 33.9, 7.1, 19.4, 27.8 and 11.8%. Licskai (Can Respir J 2012, 22891184) [AB]: ATS criteria met in 77% of sessions in a primary-care asthma program, 68% in a primary-care group practice and 91% in a hospital laboratory. Rothnie (Int J COPD 2017, 28652719) [AB]: 98.6% of primary-care traces adequate, yet a GP diagnosis of COPD matched obstruction in 72.5%; quality estimates range from 13% to 98% across studies. Population cohorts do better (BOLD, Enright, Respir Med 2011, 21549584 [AB], about 96%; CanCOLD, Tan, COPD 2014, 24666210 [AB], 95%). Error mix in 36 Chinese hospitals (Gao, Zhonghua Jie He He Hu Xi Za Zhi 2010, 20646452 [AB], 345 reports): 82.5% met start-of-test criteria, only 50.6% met end-of-test criteria; incomplete effort 14.7%, cough 8.7%, leak 8.7%, early termination 7.8%, glottic closure 2.4%. Graham 2019 [FT] cites an older occupational cohort (mean age 63) in which 53% of maneuvers lacked a plateau. Hankinson (Eur Respir J 2015, 25537554) [AB], NHANES III: the computer rejected about 16% of sessions, mainly on end-of-test, a reviewer 5.2%. Missing: any audit of hospital-laboratory errors by type under the 2019 grades.

Interpretation accuracy. Topalovic (Eur Respir J 2019, 30765505) [AB]: 120 pulmonologists, 50 cases; pattern matched ATS/ERS in 74.4% (range 56 to 88%), diagnosis correct in 44.6%, kappa 0.67 for pattern and 0.35 for diagnosis. White (Br J Gen Pract 2007, 17761057) [AB]: specialists disagreed with primary-care readers on test quality in 32% of tests (kappa 0.07), on diagnosis in 29% and on severity in 32%. van de Hei (NPJ Prim Care Respir Med 2020, 32415077) [AB]: GP versus pulmonologist diagnosis kappa 0.39 to 0.44. He (NPJ Prim Care Respir Med 2015, 25763716) [AB] and D'Urzo (Respir Care 2020, 32291310) [AB]: interpretation algorithms disagree (26 published algorithms; 92% lacked a bronchodilator prompt when the ratio was normal). Missing: trainee-versus-expert accuracy data.

## Technologist and software statements against expert re-grading

Graham 2019 Table 10 [FT]: grade A needs 3 or more acceptable maneuvers within 0.150 L; B, 2 acceptable within 0.150 L; C, 2 or more within 0.200 L; D, 2 or more within 0.250 L; E, more than 0.250 L or one acceptable; U, usable only; F, none; FEV1 and FVC are graded separately and pre- and post-bronchodilator sets separately. Topole (ERJ Open Res 2023, 36776483) [AB]: AI agreed with over-readers in 91% of 2,000 trial sessions; three physicians agreed on only 63% of 100 tests (kappa 0.5); against the physician consensus the AI agreed in 73% and the over-readers in 46%. Das (Eur Respir J 2020, 32527741) [AB]: of 36,873 NHANES 2011-12 curves, technicians labelled 54% as acceptable with satisfactory start and end, yet 93% had a usable FEV1, with high inter-technician variability. Burgos (Eur Respir J 2012, 22075488) [AB]: remote expert review beat the spirometer's automatic feedback. Perrem (Ann Am Thorac Soc 2022, 34343027) [AB]: technician agreement on a pediatric grading algorithm 92% for FEV1 and 83% for FVC. Missing: a direct audit of technologist-reported grade against an expert re-grade under the 2019 system; no published rate of wrong statements, so the simulator's frequencies are teaching choices.

## DLCO in practice

Punjabi (Chest 2003, 12684297) [AB]: of 6,193 laboratory patients 98.3% met the older reproducibility criteria. Hegewald (Respir Care 2015, 25389356) [AB]: 10 of 23 instruments inaccurate, inspired volume inaccurate in 48%. Yadav (Chest 2026, 40967368) [AB]: in 5,069 stable patients the 90th percentile of intersession change was 3.5 mL/min/mmHg (15% of predicted); "guidelines lack evidence-based thresholds" for DLCO. Hemoglobin: ARTP [FT] standardizes to 146 g/L (men) and 134 g/L (women) with the Cotes equations, and notes a 10% change above 190 or below 117 g/L; Marrades (AJRCCM 1997, 9001318) [AB]: Cotes under-corrects in anemia (slope 1.40 mL/min/mmHg per g/dL). Carboxyhemoglobin: Mohsenifar (Respiration 1979, 472498) [AB], about 1% per 1%; ARTP [FT], 1 to 2% per 1%, adjust only above 2%, five maneuvers raise COHb by about 3.5%; Mahoney (Respir Care 2007, 18028565) [AB]: 14% of non-smokers had COHb above 2%, and correction moved 2 of 100 patients down one category. Acceptability [FT, ARTP, derived from the 2017 standard]: inspired volume at least 90% of the best VC (85% reviewed with caution), 85% of the inspired volume inhaled within 4 s, breath-hold 10 +- 2 s without Valsalva or Muller, repeatability 0.67 mmol/min/kPa. Missing: frequency of unacceptable inspired volume, breath-hold or Valsalva in routine laboratories, and any data on how often hemoglobin is missing or not applied.

## Plethysmography in practice

No audit gives frequencies of unacceptable FRC. ARTP [FT]: pant at about 1 Hz (above 1.5 Hz causes error, below 0.5 Hz causes problems with the controlled leak); reviewers look for a stack of nearly superimposed lines on the mouth-pressure against box-pressure plot; hysteresis means too forceful; a leaking box seal bends the loop; cheek support reduces looping; check for drift or leak on the tidal trace before shutter closure; allow thermal equilibration; report the mean of 3 FRC values within 5%. Physiology [AB]: Rodenstein (J Appl Physiol 1982, 7085428), TGV overestimated by 1.24 +- 0.56 L with airway stenosis; Shinozaki (1991, 1753530), cheek support abolished the overestimate in severe obstruction; Bohadana (J Appl Physiol 1982, 7068490), panting frequency changes TGV.

## Interpretation errors that a challenge could target

Fixed 0.70 versus the LLN: Swanney (Thorax 2008, 18786983) [AB], 40,646 adults, GOLD false positives up to 60%, healthy never-smokers over 60 labelled obstructed in 17% to 45% of men; Roberts (Chest 2006, 16840402) [AB], 1,503 tests, 7.5% discordant, 16% in those over 74. Borderline results flip on repeat: Schermer (NPJ Prim Care Respir Med 2016, 27684728) [AB], 14.3% changed obstruction category at 1 year. Restriction from spirometry: Aaron (Chest 1999, 10084506) [AB], low FVC predicted low TLC with PPV 41%. Equation switching: Moffett (JAMA Netw Open 2023, 37261830) [AB], restriction in Black patients rose from 26.8% to 37.5% under race-neutral equations and fell in White patients from 22.6% to 18.0%; Kanj (AJRCCM 2024, 37523681) [AB], 109,447 tests, 10.2% changed category; Huprikar (Ann Am Thorac Soc 2019, 30427734) [AB], 3.2% changed obstruction status, 10.7% when severity was included. Height: Chhabra (Ann Thorac Med 2008, 19561887) [AB], arm span as height misclassified 23.7% of taller and 14.2% of shorter subjects; Kjensli (Eur Respir J 2010, 20110396) [AB], height loss overestimates lung volumes more than FEV1 or FVC; no primary study of age or sex entry errors was found. KCO: Hughes and Pride (AJRCCM 2012, 22538804) [AB], a raised KCO with low VA suggests incomplete inflation or extrapulmonary restriction. Bronchodilator response (abstracts only, conflicting): de la Serna (Respir Care 2024, 39043424), prevalence 9% to 16% when changing from the 2005 to the 2022 criterion; Li (2022, 36274994) [SEC], positivity fell from 63.3% to 52.8% in asthma and from 30.9% to 22.9% in COPD.

## Education

Braun (ATS Sch 2021, 35079740) [AB], RCT, 25 students, 2 h: instruction took knowledge from 47% to 71% and skills from 18% to 58%, self-study from 48% to 60% and 14% to 22%. Smith (BMC Med Educ 2007, 17976233) [AB], 137 students: web teaching improved spirometry interpretation more than interactive teaching. Ostrow (Am Rev Respir Dis 1975, 1096695) [AB]: guided deductive instruction beat inductive discovery. Bhatia-Lin (MedEdPORTAL 2025, 39981263) [AB]: a race-and-PFT curriculum gave Cohen's d 0.27 to 1.17. Sandelowsky (BMJ Open 2018, 30099398) [AB]: neither lecture nor case-method CME substantially improved GP spirometry interpretation. Das (Eur Respir J 2023, 37080566) [AB]: explainable-AI support raised pulmonologist accuracy by 10.4% (phase 1) and 5.4% (phase 2). Training and feedback raised test quality (Eaton; Burgos). Missing: any study of deliberate practice with feedback, error detection or pattern-recognition curricula for PFTs.

## Trend table

ARTP [FT]: spirometry varies up to 200 mL on repeat; cross-sectional z-scores are unsuitable for monitoring because of cohort effects; show serial absolute values; estimating true decline needs 5 to 10 years. Robson (Thorax 2001, 11312403) [AB]: coefficient of repeatability for TLCO +-1.60 mmol/min/kPa and for KCO +-0.24. Hnizdo (Occup Environ Med 2005, 16169915) [AB]: within-person FEV1 SD 122 to 177 mL in workplace programs. Jensen (Chest 2007, 17400686) [AB]: instruments account for 13% to 58% of FEV1 variability and 36% to 70% of DLCO variability. The 2005 percentages for FEV1 (normal subjects: within-day 5%, week-to-week 11%, year-to-year 15%; COPD within-day 13%, week-to-week 20%) are [SEC] only. The 2022 standard's abstract says it updates "limits of natural changes over time"; secondary sources say it centers on FEV1Q and a pediatric conditional change score with no DLCO or FVC thresholds; its actual numbers are unverified. No change threshold used by the simulator may be called a guideline value until the primary text is read.
